How trip expense splitting works

The mistake most groups make is trying to settle each expense on its own. Six people, ten expenses, and suddenly everyone owes everyone a little. The fix is to net everything out first.

  1. Total what each person paid across all expenses.
  2. Total what each person's share comes to, counting only the expenses they actually shared.
  3. Net = paid − share. Positive means the group owes them; negative means they owe the group.
  4. Match the biggest debtor to the biggest creditor, repeatedly, until everyone is at zero.
Net = what you paid − what you owe. The nets always sum to zero. If they don't, an expense has been entered twice.

Why the last step matters

Netting alone still leaves you sending money in every direction. Matching biggest-to-biggest is what collapses it. In a group of six there are fifteen possible pairs of people; a settled trip usually needs five transfers or fewer. Same money, far less UPI admin, and much less chance of someone forgetting a ₹200 payment.

Expenses only some people shared

Not everything is shared by the whole group. Two of you take a taxi to the airport early; three of you skip the scuba diving. Untick the people who did not share an expense and only the rest are charged for it.

This is where a spreadsheet starts to hurt. Every partially-shared expense needs its own divisor, and one wrong row quietly throws off everyone's balance with nothing to warn you.

Frequently asked questions

How do you split expenses on a group trip?
Record each expense with its amount, who paid, and who shared it. Then net each person's payments against their share and settle between the positive and negative balances.
What does simplifying debts mean?
Netting everything out and matching the biggest debtor to the biggest creditor, so the group makes the fewest possible transfers instead of everyone repaying everyone.
What if one person did not share an expense?
Untick them for that expense. Only the people who shared it are charged.
