How day-wise rent splitting works

Day-wise splitting charges each person in proportion to the days they were actually present, instead of dividing the bill into equal parts. The formula is:

Your share = (your days ÷ total person-days) × total amount

Total person-days is the sum of everyone's days, not the number of days in the month. That distinction matters — it is the single most common mistake people make doing this by hand.

Worked example

Three flatmates share ₹12,000 of costs for a 30-day month. Asha and Bhavin were there all 30 days; Chetan moved in on the 20th, so he was present 11 days.

  • Total person-days = 30 + 30 + 11 = 71
  • Asha = (30 ÷ 71) × ₹12,000 = ₹5,070.42
  • Bhavin = (30 ÷ 71) × ₹12,000 = ₹5,070.42
  • Chetan = (11 ÷ 71) × ₹12,000 = ₹1,859.15

Split equally instead and Chetan would pay ₹4,000 for eleven days — roughly ₹2,140 more than his fair share. That gap is why day-wise matters.

Equal or day-wise — which should you use?

They are not interchangeable, and the honest answer is that most flatmates need both in the same month.

Use an equal split for rent itself

A room is reserved for you whether you sleep in it or not. If everyone holds their room for the full month, the landlord charges for the room, not for attendance — so an equal split is the fair one. Prorating rent because someone spent a fortnight at their parents' place is usually the wrong call.

Use a day-wise split for consumed costs

Groceries, cooking gas, vegetables, and electricity are consumed only when someone is actually there. These should follow days present. The same applies to rent when a flatmate genuinely joined or left partway through the month — they held the room for part of the period, so they owe part of the rent.

A practical rule: fixed costs equal, consumed costs day-wise.

Frequently asked questions

How do you split rent when one flatmate stays fewer days?
Use a day-wise split. Add up the days everyone actually stayed to get total person-days, then charge each person (their days ÷ total person-days) × the rent.
Should rent be split equally or day-wise?
Equally when everyone holds a room all month, since the room is reserved regardless. Day-wise for consumed costs like groceries and electricity, and for anyone who joined or left mid-month.
